02

About

I am a third-year undergraduate at MIT studying Artificial Intelligence and Decision Making, graduating in May 2028. I am interested in how we evaluate AI systems and whether those evaluations hold up over time, along with machine learning, applications of AI, and AI safety. My coursework includes machine learning, algorithms, probability and statistics, dynamical systems and control, AI and society, and the law of artificial intelligence and big data.

Since February 2025, I have been an undergraduate research assistant at MIT FutureTech in CSAIL. My main project studies the reproducibility of LLM behavioral evaluations: I reproduced the ELEPHANT sycophancy benchmark across 11 models and 6 model families, and showed how its findings depend on the choice of judge model and on model deprecation. I also prompt-engineer LLMs to extract evidence of AI job automation from more than 200,000 excerpts of research papers, and help maintain a database of how AI is used across scientific research.

I also enjoy building things quickly. At HackMIT, I have co-built an AI résumé and job-matching app and Unwrap, a social shopping and group-gifting app powered by Claude. Outside of academics, I choreograph and teach original routines for DanceTroupe, where I lead rehearsals and help keep the team close-knit.

Current Research

Reproducibility and LLM Evaluation

Reproducing Behavioral Evaluations of Large Language Models

I am studying the behavior of LLM sycophancy, conducting a reproduction study of behavioral evaluations of large language models. This work involves reproducing model outputs, applying evaluation methods, comparing results across models and providers, and analyzing sources of variation in experimental findings.

A condensed version of this work, Kristina Sakayeva and Ana Trišović, “Reproducing ELEPHANT: Judge-Dependence and Model Deprecation in an LLM Sycophancy Benchmark,” has been accepted at the TAE Workshop @ NeurIPS 2026 (Sydney) and the AI4MetaScience Workshop @ NeurIPS 2026 (Paris). Code is available on GitHub.

An extended version is currently in revision at TMLR.

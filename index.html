About

I am a third-year undergraduate at MIT studying Artificial Intelligence and Decision Making. My academic interests include machine learning, applications of AI, and questions surrounding AI risks and safety. My coursework spans programming, data science, discrete mathematics, linear algebra, algorithms, machine learning, educational technology, and the legal implications of artificial intelligence and big data. At HackMIT, I helped build an AI-powered web application that extracts evidence-backed skill profiles from résumés, research papers, and project descriptions. I also integrated and prompt-engineered an AI agent to match users’ skills and preferences with job postings, producing ranked recommendations based on preference similarity and skill fit. Outside of academics, I am involved in dance as a choreographer and team leader. I create and teach original routines, coordinate rehearsals and formations, and provide individualized feedback to support both dancer development and overall performance quality. Through this role, I have strengthened my leadership, communication, and teamwork skills while helping build a supportive and cohesive group environment.

As an undergraduate researcher at MIT FutureTech, I have contributed to research on the use of artificial intelligence in science. My work has included building research datasets, conducting literature reviews, organizing and validating data, and using language models to extract structured information from scientific text.

Current Research

Reproducibility and LLM Evaluation

Reproducing Behavioral Evaluations of Large Language Models

I am studying the behavior of LLM sycophancy, conducting a reproduction study of behavioral evaluations of large language models. This work involves reproducing model outputs, applying evaluation methods, comparing results across models and providers, and analyzing sources of variation in experimental findings.

A condensed version of this work, Kristina Sakayeva and Ana Trišović, “Reproducing ELEPHANT: Judge-Dependence and Model Deprecation in an LLM Sycophancy Benchmark,” has been accepted at the TAE Workshop @ NeurIPS 2026 (Sydney) and the AI4MetaScience Workshop @ NeurIPS 2026 (Paris). Code is available on GitHub.

An extended version is under review at TMLR.
